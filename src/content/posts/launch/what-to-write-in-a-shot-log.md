## The core fields worth including every time

Date, dose (mg and units drawn), injection site and zone, and weight if you're tracking it. These four fields take under a minute to fill in and cover the essentials most people want to look back on.

### Why units, not just mg

Writing down both the mg dose and the units actually drawn creates a cross-check: if the units don't match what the mg dose and current concentration should produce, it's a prompt to double-check the maths before the next dose, not just after.

## Optional but useful fields

A short note on side effects that week, energy levels, or anything unusual (travel, illness, a missed dose) adds context that's easy to forget by the time you look back a few months later.

### Keeping notes short

A single line, "mild nausea day 2, gone by day 4," is more useful in practice than a paragraph, since it's actually likely to get written every week rather than skipped when time is short.

## A simple weekly template

Week / Date / Dose (mg) / Units drawn / Site / Weight / Note. Six fields, one line, repeated weekly. Simple enough to sustain over months, detailed enough to be genuinely useful later.
