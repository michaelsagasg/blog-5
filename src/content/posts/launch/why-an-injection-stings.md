## A mild sting is common, not unusual

A brief sting or burning sensation during or right after an injection is commonly reported and usually fades within seconds to a minute. It's generally attributed to the solution itself, its pH or concentration, rather than anything being wrong with the technique.

### Cold solution is a frequent factor

Injecting a solution straight from the fridge is one of the more commonly cited reasons for a stronger sting. Letting the vial warm slightly at room temperature for a few minutes before drawing a dose is a common, simple adjustment people make.

## Concentration and volume

A more concentrated solution, or a larger injected volume, can sting more than a more dilute one delivering the same dose. This is one of several practical reasons some people choose a slightly more diluted reconstitution ratio.

### Injection speed and technique

Injecting too quickly, or into a spot that's been used very recently, are both commonly reported to increase discomfort. A slow, steady injection into a fresh site tends to sting less.

## When it's worth noting rather than ignoring

A sting that's unusually severe, lasts well beyond a minute, or is accompanied by significant swelling or redness spreading beyond the injection site itself is worth logging clearly and paying attention to over the following days, rather than dismissing as routine.
