## Lipohypertrophy, the main long-term concern

Repeatedly injecting the same small area can lead to lipohypertrophy, a build-up of thickened, lumpy tissue under the skin. Beyond being a cosmetic concern, it can also affect how consistently a drug is absorbed at that site, since scarred or thickened tissue doesn't absorb medication the same way healthy tissue does.

### Why this matters for dosing consistency

If a dose is consistently injected into an affected area without realising it, absorption may become less predictable, which could show up as inconsistent effects from week to week without an obvious cause, since the dose itself hasn't changed.

## It builds up slowly, which is why it's easy to miss

Lipohypertrophy typically develops gradually, over months of repeated use at the same site, not from a single injection. That slow build-up is exactly why it's easy to overlook without a deliberate rotation habit and periodic self-checks of injection areas.

### Checking your own sites periodically

Feeling for any firm, lumpy, or numb areas at commonly used injection sites every few weeks is a simple habit that catches this early, well before it becomes a significant area to avoid entirely.

## The connection to logging

A rotation log doesn't just prevent the problem; it also makes it possible to identify which site might be affected if something does turn up, since the record shows exactly where and how often each site has actually been used.
