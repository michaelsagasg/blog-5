## Why spares matter more than the bare minimum

Buying exactly enough syringes for a course, one per planned dose and nothing more, leaves no room for a needle that bends while drawing from a vial, dulls from being recapped incorrectly, or gets contaminated by an accidental touch. Buying 20% to 30% more than the exact course length, so roughly 15 to 16 syringes for a 12-week course, covers these situations without needing an emergency pharmacy trip.

### How needles bend or dull in normal use

A needle tip can dull slightly just from passing through a rubber vial stopper more than once, which is why drawing from the vial and then injecting with the same needle, rather than switching to a fresh one for injection, is worth avoiding where a spare is available. Bending usually happens from an awkward angle while drawing, or from being knocked against something while uncapped.

## What to do with a compromised needle

A bent, dulled, or contaminated needle should be discarded straight into the sharps bin rather than used, since a bent tip causes more discomfort going in and a dulled or contaminated one carries more risk than it's worth for the sake of saving one syringe.

### Recognising a needle that shouldn't be used

Checking the needle briefly before injecting, looking for a visible bend or a tip that looks anything other than straight, takes a couple of seconds and catches the obvious problems before they cause discomfort.

## Buying enough without overbuying

A box of 100 insulin syringes, bought online from a UK medical supplier, comfortably covers a 12-week course with spares to use for drawing separately from injecting, and still leaves enough for a second course afterwards. This is usually cheaper per unit than buying the exact number needed from a pharmacy counter in smaller packs.

### Where to buy in the UK

Pharmacies sell packs of 10 for quick, immediate need. Online medical suppliers sell boxes of 100 at a better per-unit price for anyone planning ahead for a full course. Checking what a peptide listing already includes before adding a separate order avoids ending up with far more than needed.

## Keeping track of spares in the log

Noting when a spare needle was used, and why, in the log's notes column, even just "needle bent while drawing, used spare," helps build a picture over time of whether this is a recurring issue worth addressing, perhaps through a technique adjustment, rather than a one-off.

## A simple technique that reduces bending

Drawing from a vial at a straight angle, rather than at a slant, and avoiding pressing the needle tip against the bottom of the vial while drawing the last of a dose, are two small technique habits that noticeably reduce how often a needle bends. Neither takes extra time once it becomes routine, and both reduce how many spares actually get used across a course.

### Recapping needles safely

If a needle needs to be recapped between drawing and injecting, using a one-handed scoop technique rather than trying to guide the cap on with the other hand reduces both the risk of an accidental needle-stick and the chance of dulling the tip against the cap's edge.

## When to treat a repeated pattern as worth addressing

If bent or dulled needles are showing up more than once every few weeks, that's worth a closer look at drawing technique rather than simply buying more spares indefinitely. Slowing down the drawing motion, angling the syringe more carefully into the vial's stopper, and avoiding pressing the needle against the vial's base usually resolves a recurring pattern within a week or two of deliberate attention.

### A final word on cost versus hassle

The extra few pounds spent on a slightly larger box of syringes is small compared with the hassle of an unplanned pharmacy trip on the exact day a dose is due, making spare needles one of the easiest cost-benefit decisions on the whole supply list.

## A short buying checklist

Buy 20% to 30% more syringes than the exact course length requires, check each needle briefly before injecting, discard anything bent, dulled, or contaminated straight into the sharps bin, and buy in bulk online for the best value if planning more than one course ahead.
