## What to look for on the label

Topical numbing creams sold over the counter in UK pharmacies typically contain lidocaine at around 4% to 5%, the active ingredient that numbs the skin's surface. Products like this are sold under various brand names at Boots, Lloyds and larger supermarkets with a pharmacy counter, usually priced between £6 and £15 depending on tube size and brand.

### How it's meant to be used

Numbing cream is applied to the intended injection site and left for the time stated on the packaging, commonly 20 to 30 minutes, before the area is wiped clean and the injection given. Applying it too briefly won't give the numbing effect time to take hold, so checking the stated wait time before planning the injection around it matters.

## Is it worth adding to the routine

For subcutaneous injections with a fine-gauge needle, many people find the injection itself only mildly noticeable without any numbing cream, so it's not something everyone needs. For anyone particularly sensitive to the sensation, or just starting out and feeling anxious about it, numbing cream is a reasonable thing to try and can make the first few weeks more comfortable while the routine becomes familiar.

### A patch test is worth doing first

Applying a small amount to a patch of skin a day or two before using it on an injection site checks for any skin reaction. This is a sensible precaution before relying on numbing cream as part of a weekly routine.

## Where to buy it in the UK

Pharmacy counters stock numbing creams for general topical use, not exclusively marketed for peptide injections but suitable for the purpose. Online pharmacies and general retailers sell the same products, sometimes at a better price for larger tube sizes, which last longer given how little is needed per application.

### Alternatives worth knowing about

A simple ice cube held against the site for a minute or two before injecting has a similar numbing effect for some people and costs nothing. This is worth trying before spending on a cream, particularly for anyone unsure whether numbing is something they'll want to use regularly.

## Fitting it into a weekly log routine

If numbing cream is used, applying it needs to happen 20 to 30 minutes before the injection, which means factoring that time into whenever the weekly dose is planned. Noting in the log whether numbing cream was used, and how the injection felt that week, builds a useful comparison over time against weeks it wasn't used.

## How long a single tube lasts

A standard 30g tube of numbing cream, using a small pea-sized amount per application, typically lasts well over a full 12-week course when used once weekly. This makes it one of the cheaper ongoing supplies on a shopping list, even though the upfront tube price looks higher than a box of swabs or syringes.

### Checking for known allergies first

Anyone with a known sensitivity to local anaesthetics, or a general history of skin reactions to topical products, should check the ingredient list carefully and consider asking a pharmacist before first use, rather than relying on a patch test alone if there's already a known risk.

### Storing an opened tube correctly

Once opened, a numbing cream tube should be kept at room temperature, away from direct heat, and used within the timeframe stated on the packaging, generally several months. Checking the expiry date periodically over a long course avoids applying a product past its effective shelf life without realising.

### A final practical note

Numbing cream is one of the more optional items on a supply list, worth trying if comfort is a genuine concern, but not something every logger needs to buy by default.

### One last reminder

A more comfortable routine is more likely to be a sustainable one, so it's worth trying whatever small adjustments, numbing cream included, make the weekly injection feel easier to stick with over months.

## A short buying checklist

A cream with 4% to 5% lidocaine from a UK pharmacy or online retailer, a patch test done before first use, the stated wait time factored into the weekly routine, and a note in the log about whether it was used, so any comfort difference shows up clearly over a few weeks rather than being a vague impression.
