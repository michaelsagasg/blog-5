## Look for 70% isopropyl alcohol

Standard injection swabs are individually wrapped pads soaked in 70% isopropyl alcohol, the concentration most commonly used to clean an injection site before a subcutaneous dose. UK pharmacies sell boxes of 100 for around £2 to £5, making them one of the cheapest supplies on any injection shopping list. Checking the packaging for "70% isopropyl alcohol" is enough to confirm a box is the right type.

### Individually wrapped vs bulk pads

Individually wrapped swabs are more convenient for a weekly routine since one sealed pad covers one injection, with no risk of the alcohol drying out between uses. Bulk cotton pads soaked from a shared bottle work too and are cheaper per use, but they need to be used fresh each time rather than left out, which is more effort for a small saving.

## Where to buy them in the UK

Every major UK pharmacy chain, Boots, Lloyds, Superdrug, stocks alcohol swabs on the shelf, no prescription required. Supermarkets with a pharmacy counter often carry them too. Online, medical supply retailers sell larger boxes of 200 or more at a lower per-swab price, with typical UK delivery of two to four working days.

### What some sellers include

A number of UK peptide listings bundle a small pack of swabs with a starter order, alongside syringes and a sharps bin. Reading the listing's included-items section before buying a separate box avoids duplicate purchases in the first week.

## How swabs fit into a logging routine

The swab step happens right before the injection itself: wipe the chosen site, let it air dry for a few seconds rather than blowing on it, then inject. Some loggers note the injection site in their weekly entry at the same moment they reach for the swab, since doing both together as one motion is easier to remember than treating them as separate steps.

### Letting the site dry matters more than people expect

Injecting into a site while the alcohol is still wet can sting more than injecting into a dry site, since the alcohol gets carried under the skin with the needle. Waiting five to ten seconds after wiping, roughly the time it takes to draw the dose into the syringe, is usually enough for the site to dry properly.

## Buying enough for a full course

A 12-week course logged weekly needs at least 12 swabs, but a box of 100 lasts most people well over a year even with the odd extra swab used for a site that needs a second wipe. There's rarely a reason to buy anything larger than a single box at a time.

## What to do if you run out mid-course

Running out of swabs partway through a course is a minor inconvenience rather than a real problem, since any UK pharmacy sells them without a prescription for a same-day purchase. In a pinch, a cotton pad and a small amount of rubbing alcohol from a first aid kit works as a temporary substitute, though this isn't as convenient or as consistently dosed as a proper individually wrapped swab.

### Skin sensitivity to alcohol

A small number of people find repeated alcohol swabbing causes mild skin dryness or irritation at the injection sites over a long course. If this happens, switching to a fragrance-free alcohol wipe, or in rare cases an alternative antiseptic recommended by a pharmacist, is worth discussing with a pharmacist rather than skipping the cleaning step altogether.

### Buying enough to never run short

Since swabs are inexpensive and don't expire quickly, erring on the side of buying a slightly larger box than the exact course requires removes any risk of running short and having to substitute something less consistent on injection day.

## A short checklist

70% isopropyl alcohol on the label, individually wrapped pads for convenience, bought from a pharmacy for speed or online in bulk for cost, and checked against what a peptide listing already includes before ordering a separate box. Keeping the box next to the rest of the injection supplies, close to the sharps bin and the log, means the whole routine from prep to disposal stays in one place rather than scattered across different drawers.

None of this needs to be expensive or complicated, but having swabs ready before the first dose is due removes one more thing to think about on injection day.
