## Water retention is the most common cause

Weight can shift by a kilo or more within a week purely from water retention, driven by sodium intake, carbohydrate intake, hormonal changes, or even the weather. None of this reflects a change in actual fat mass, but it shows up on the scale exactly the same as if it did, which is why a single week's number can be misleading on its own.

### Why carbohydrate intake matters here

Carbohydrates are stored in the body alongside water, so a higher-carbohydrate day or two before a weigh-in can add water weight that has nothing to do with fat gain. A lower-carbohydrate stretch can have the opposite effect, showing a drop that's partly water rather than fat loss.

## Digestive contents add variability too

The food and waste physically present in the digestive system at the moment of weighing can account for a noticeable swing, particularly if a weigh-in happens at a different time of day than usual, or after a larger meal the evening before. This is why keeping the weigh-in time consistent matters as much as the frequency.

### Hormonal cycles

For anyone who menstruates, hormonal fluctuation across the cycle is a well-documented cause of temporary water retention, sometimes adding a kilo or two in the days before a period, unrelated to fat mass.

## GLP-1 specific factors

Appetite changes reported on GLP-1 peptides can shift eating patterns week to week, which indirectly affects the numbers on the scale beyond the underlying trend. Gastrointestinal changes, also commonly reported, can affect digestive contents at the time of weighing in ways that add further noise to a single week's reading.

### Why this makes a written log more useful, not less

A single week's fluctuation is hard to interpret in isolation, but a written log covering several weeks in a row starts to separate genuine trend from noise, since the noise tends to average out over time while a real trend continues in one direction.

## How to read a fluctuating log without overreacting

Looking at the overall direction across four or more weeks, rather than the week-to-week change alone, is a more reliable way to judge progress. A single week showing no change, or even a small increase, isn't unusual and doesn't necessarily mean anything has gone wrong with the underlying trend.

## A worked example of a fluctuating week

Imagine a log showing 80.1kg, then 80.4kg the following week, then 79.6kg the week after. Read in isolation, the middle week looks like a setback. Read as part of a three-week stretch, the overall direction is clearly downward, and the middle week's small rise is well within the range explained by normal water retention or a heavier meal the night before weighing in.

### Why this example matters for reading your own log

This is exactly the kind of pattern that causes people to lose confidence in a log that's actually tracking normally. Recognising this shape in advance, and expecting it to happen periodically, makes it far easier to stay calm about a single up week when it eventually shows up in a real log.

## Exercise and its effect on short-term readings

Starting or significantly increasing exercise can cause temporary water retention in the days that follow, as muscles retain fluid during recovery, which can mask fat loss on the scale for a week or two even while overall health markers are improving. This is a well-documented, temporary effect rather than a sign that exercise isn't helping.

### A final practical note

Understanding these causes in advance makes a single odd week far less alarming when it eventually shows up in a real log, which it almost certainly will at some point.

### One last reminder

Normal fluctuation is a feature of how bodies work, not a flaw in a course or a log. Expecting it in advance makes it far easier to take in stride.

## The bottom line

Week-to-week fluctuation on GLP-1 peptides is normal and expected, driven mostly by water retention, digestive contents, and hormonal factors rather than the peptide itself. A log kept consistently over several weeks reads far more clearly than any single entry taken alone.
