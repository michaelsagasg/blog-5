## The injection supplies

Beyond the tirzepatide itself, a first week needs bacteriostatic water if it's sold separately, insulin syringes sized correctly (0.5ml with a 29 to 31 gauge needle suits most early doses), alcohol swabs, and a sharps bin. Having all of this ready before the first vial or pen arrives avoids any delay once the first dose is due.

### Checking what's already included

A number of UK tirzepatide listings bundle syringes and swabs with the order itself. Checking the listing's included-items section before buying separately saves ending up with duplicates in the first week, which is easy to do when ordering everything in a rush.

## The reconstitution details

If tirzepatide arrives as a powder needing reconstitution, knowing the water volume to add and the resulting concentration in advance means the first dose can be drawn correctly as soon as everything arrives, rather than working out the maths with the vial already sat open.

### Where this information comes from

A listing with clearly stated, COA-backed mg content and suggested reconstitution guidance removes most of the uncertainty for a first-time buyer. If a listing doesn't provide reconstitution guidance directly, working the calculation out ahead of time, before the vial arrives, avoids errors on the day itself.

## The tracking setup

A notebook, spreadsheet or app, set up with the core fields, date, dose, site, weight, ready before the first entry is due, means the first week starts as a complete record rather than something filled in retroactively. The specific format matters less than choosing one and sticking with it for at least the first month.

### A scale worth trusting

If weight tracking is part of the plan, a scale that reads consistently on a hard, level surface is worth more than one with extra functions. A quick check, two readings a minute apart, confirms whether it's reliable enough to trust for weekly comparisons.

## A short pre-start checklist

Tirzepatide and reconstitution supplies sourced and confirmed. Syringes, swabs and a sharps bin ready, cross-checked against what the order already includes. A tracking format chosen and set up with its fields in place. A rough sense of the first few weeks' dosing pattern, drawn from the listing's guidance where one is given. Sorting each of these before the first dose is due turns week one into a complete log entry rather than a catch-up exercise.

## A realistic total cost for week one

Budgeting for a first week means looking beyond the tirzepatide itself. A rough UK total for supporting supplies, syringes, swabs, a sharps bin, and bacteriostatic water if not already included, typically lands somewhere between £20 and £40, depending on whether items are bought individually or as part of a bundle. Knowing this figure ahead of time avoids the first order feeling more expensive than expected once every supporting item is added up.

### Spreading the cost across a course

Since most of these supplies, aside from consumable syringes and swabs, are one-off purchases, this cost is front-loaded into the first week and doesn't repeat across the rest of a 12-week course. Thinking of it as a one-time setup cost rather than a recurring weekly expense gives a clearer picture of the overall budget.

## Double-checking the order before it's placed

Running through the full list once more before confirming an order, peptide, syringes, swabs, sharps bin, water if needed, and the tracking format, catches most gaps before they turn into a problem on the actual injection day rather than afterwards.

### A final practical note

None of this needs to happen in a single afternoon. Spreading the sourcing across a few days, as long as everything's ready before the first dose is due, is perfectly reasonable.

### One last reminder

A well-prepared first week isn't about perfection, it's about having enough ready that the actual routine can start smoothly and the log can begin on time.

## Why the first week matters most

A first week where every supply is already in hand and the tracking format is already decided tends to carry momentum into the weeks after it. Most logging habits that stall do so because something wasn't ready at the start, a missing supply, an undecided format, not because keeping the log itself was too much effort.
