## The injection supplies

Beyond the retatrutide itself, a first week needs bacteriostatic water if it isn't already included, insulin syringes in the right size (0.5ml with a 29 to 31 gauge needle suits most early doses), alcohol swabs, and a sharps disposal bin. Buying all of these before the first vial arrives means there's no delay once dosing day comes around.

### Checking what's already included

Many UK retatrutide listings bundle some of these items, syringes and swabs most commonly, with the order. Reading the listing's included-items section before buying separately avoids duplicate purchases in the first week, which is an easy mistake to make when ordering everything at once.

## The reconstitution details

If the retatrutide arrives as a lyophilised powder needing reconstitution, knowing the water volume to add and the resulting concentration before the vial arrives means the first dose can be drawn correctly the moment everything's in hand, rather than pausing to work out the maths with the vial already open.

### Where this information comes from

A COA-backed listing that states the mg content clearly, ideally alongside suggested reconstitution guidance, removes most of the uncertainty. Where a listing doesn't give reconstitution guidance directly, working out the maths in advance, before the vial arrives, avoids getting it wrong on the day.

## The tracking setup

A notebook, spreadsheet, or app, set up with the basic fields, date, dose, site, weight, before the first entry is due, means week one starts complete rather than half-formed. Choosing a format now and sticking with it for at least the first month matters more than which specific format is chosen.

### A scale worth trusting

If weight is part of the log, a scale that gives a consistent reading on a hard, level surface matters more than one with extra features. Testing it with two readings a minute apart before the first proper weigh-in confirms it's reliable enough to trust week after week.

## A short pre-start checklist

Retatrutide and reconstitution supplies sourced and confirmed. Syringes, swabs and a sharps bin ready, checked against what the order already includes. A tracking format chosen and set up with its basic fields in place. A rough idea of the first few weeks' dosing, based on the listing's guidance if it provides one. Getting each of these sorted before the first dose is due means the first logged week is a complete entry, not a scramble to catch up on missing supplies.

## A realistic total cost for week one

Budgeting for a first week means accounting for more than just the retatrutide itself. A rough UK total for supplies alone, syringes, swabs, a sharps bin, and bacteriostatic water if not included, typically comes to somewhere between £20 and £40 depending on whether items are bought individually or as a bundle. Knowing this figure in advance avoids a first order feeling more expensive than expected once every supporting supply is added up.

### Spreading the cost across a course

Since most of these supplies, aside from consumable syringes and swabs, are one-off purchases, this initial cost is front-loaded into week one and doesn't repeat for the rest of a 12-week course. Framing it as a one-time setup cost, rather than a recurring weekly expense, gives a more accurate picture of the overall budget for the log.

## Double-checking the order before it's placed

Running through the full list once more before clicking confirm, peptide, syringes, swabs, sharps bin, water if needed, and the tracking format, catches most gaps before they become a problem on the actual injection day rather than after.

### A final practical note

None of these steps need to happen in a single afternoon. Spreading the sourcing across a few days, while still finishing before the first dose is due, is perfectly reasonable.

## Why the first week sets the tone

A first week that starts with every supply already in hand and a log format already decided tends to be easier to keep up in the following weeks too. Most log-keeping habits that fall away do so because something wasn't ready on day one, a missing syringe size, an undecided tracking format, rather than because the habit itself was too demanding.
