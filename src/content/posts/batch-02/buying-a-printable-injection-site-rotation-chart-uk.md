## Free templates versus bought charts

A printable injection site rotation chart is available for free from a range of health information sites, showing the common subcutaneous zones, abdomen, thighs, and upper arms, with a grid or numbered pattern to work through. Some UK stationery retailers and online sellers also offer bought versions, printed on sturdier card and sometimes laminated for repeated use with a wipe-clean marker, typically £5 to £10.

### What makes a chart actually useful

A good chart shows enough distinct spots to avoid reusing the same point too often, usually a grid of eight or more zones per body area, and has clear space to mark the date each spot was used. A chart too vague, just "left side, right side" with no further detail, doesn't give enough resolution to track rotation properly over months.

## Where to buy or find one in the UK

Free templates are widely available as printable downloads. For a sturdier version, general stationery shops, some pharmacies, and online marketplaces stock laminated rotation charts, sometimes sold alongside injection log journals as a bundle.

### Laminated versus paper

A laminated chart used with a wipe-clean marker lasts the length of a whole course without wearing out, and can be reset for a new course afterwards. A paper chart is cheaper and fine for a single course, but needs replacing or photocopying if it's needed again later.

## How to use it week to week

Marking the site used immediately after each injection, right when the rest of that week's log entry is being written, keeps the chart accurate rather than relying on memory to fill it in later. Working through the chart's zones in sequence, rather than picking at random, gives the most even rotation over time.

### Combining it with the weekly log

Writing the site used in the weekly log entry itself, alongside marking it on the chart, gives two records that cross-check each other. If the chart shows a gap in one area, that's a sign to favour that zone in the coming weeks.

## Why bother with a chart at all

Rotating sites is generally recommended to avoid repeated use of the same small spot, and a chart makes the difference between an intentional rotation and a rough guess based on memory. For the cost of a printed page, it removes one more thing to think hard about each week.

## Multi-course charts

For anyone planning more than one course, a laminated chart that can be wiped clean and reused, or a template saved digitally and reprinted fresh for each new course, avoids needing to buy a new one every few months. Keeping old completed charts on file, even just as a photo, is also a useful reference for how rotation was handled during a previous course.

### A chart alone isn't a complete record

A rotation chart works best as a companion to the weekly log rather than a replacement for it. The chart shows the pattern of sites used; the log's own site column provides the dated, chronological record that ties each specific site back to a specific week's dose and any notes from that week.

## Adjusting a chart to personal comfort

Not every zone on a generic chart will feel equally comfortable to use for everyone. If a particular spot consistently causes more discomfort, it's reasonable to use it less often than the chart's strict rotation would suggest, favouring other zones slightly more, as long as the overall pattern still avoids overusing any single point. A chart is a guide for even rotation, not a rule that has to be followed with mathematical precision every single week.

### Sharing a chart isn't necessary

A rotation chart is a personal tracking tool and doesn't need to be shown to anyone else unless it's useful context for a healthcare conversation. Keeping it private, in the same folder or drawer as the rest of the log, is completely normal.

## A short buying checklist

A chart with at least eight distinct zones per body area, dated marking space, laminated if planning to reuse it across a full course, and kept next to the weekly log so marking it becomes part of the same short routine rather than a separate task.
