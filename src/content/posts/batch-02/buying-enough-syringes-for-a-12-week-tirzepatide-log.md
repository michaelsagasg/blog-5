## The baseline number

A 12-week tirzepatide log with one injection per week needs at least 12 syringes as a bare minimum. Buying more than that is sensible in practice, since a bent needle, a spoiled draw, or a second injection needed after adjusting a reconstitution can use up a spare unexpectedly. A realistic number for a smooth 12-week course is closer to 15 or 20.

### Why extra syringes are worth the small cost

A syringe that's been touched at the needle tip, bent while drawing from a vial, or otherwise compromised needs to be thrown away rather than reused. Without spares already on hand, this becomes an unplanned pharmacy trip on injection day, which a few extra syringes bought upfront avoids entirely.

## Where to buy them in the UK

UK pharmacies sell packs of 10 over the counter without a prescription, priced from around £5 to £10. Online medical suppliers sell boxes of 100 at a lower per-unit cost, enough to cover several 12-week courses in a single order and skip the need to reorder mid-course. For one 12-week log, a box of 100 is more than sufficient and considerably cheaper per unit than buying repeated small packs.

### Getting the size right

Tirzepatide doses, particularly in the earlier weeks of a titration schedule, are usually small volumes, so a 0.5ml syringe with a 29 to 31 gauge needle reads more clearly than a 1ml syringe. Confirming the size matches the expected dose before buying in bulk avoids being stuck with the wrong size for the whole course.

## Matching supply to the log

Buying syringes in one batch at the start of the 12 weeks means the same size and gauge is used throughout, which keeps log entries consistent from the first week to the last. If a size change does happen partway through, noting it in the log explains any difference in how the dose appears on the page.

### Counting supplies out weekly

Counting out a week's worth of syringes into a small container at the start of each week, alongside swabs and the sharps bin, makes it easy to notice at a glance when stock is getting low, well before it runs out entirely.

## What some tirzepatide listings include

Some UK tirzepatide sellers bundle a starter supply of syringes with a first order. Checking a listing's included-items section before buying a separate box for the full 12-week course avoids ending up with more syringes than needed early on.

## What to do with leftover syringes

If a course ends with syringes still remaining, whether from a bulk box or a course that finished sooner than planned, they're not wasted. Unopened, individually wrapped syringes generally keep for years in dry storage, so any leftover stock is simply a head start on a future course, or worth a periodic check of the use-by date if there's no immediate plan to use them.

### Disposing of unused stock safely

An unused syringe doesn't carry the same contamination risk as a used one, so it doesn't need to go through the sharps disposal process, though it should still be disposed of responsibly rather than thrown loose in general rubbish. Most pharmacies will accept unopened stock back if asked.

### Storing syringes properly

Keeping unopened syringes in their original packaging, somewhere cool and dry and away from direct sunlight, preserves their sterility for the manufacturer's stated shelf life, usually several years. A humid bathroom cabinet is a less ideal storage spot than a bedroom drawer or a dedicated supply box kept elsewhere in the house.

### Sharing supplies within a household

If more than one person in a household is running a course, keeping each person's syringes and other supplies in clearly separate, labelled containers avoids any mix-up, particularly if different gauge or barrel sizes are being used for different doses.

## A short buying checklist

Buy 15 to 20 syringes for a 12-week course to cover spares, choose 0.5ml syringes with a 29 to 31 gauge needle for typical dose volumes, check what the tirzepatide listing already includes before ordering separately, and keep the size and gauge consistent throughout the course so the log stays easy to compare week to week.
