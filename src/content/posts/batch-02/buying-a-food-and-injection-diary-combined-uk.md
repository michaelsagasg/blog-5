## What a combined diary adds

A combined food and injection diary puts meal notes, dose entries, injection site, and weight on the same page, rather than keeping two separate records. UK stationery retailers and online sellers offer dedicated combined diaries from around £10 to £18, or a simple printable template does the same job for free.

### Why combining them can be useful

Appetite changes are one of the most commonly reported effects logged by people using GLP-1 peptides, and having food notes next to the dose entry makes it easier to spot a pattern, reduced appetite in the days right after a dose, for example, without flipping between two separate books or apps.

## What fields a good combined diary includes

At minimum: date, dose and injection site, a rough note on appetite or meals that day, and the weekly weight entry. Some combined diaries add a side effects column too, which is worth having even if it's left blank most weeks, since a single unusual entry is far more useful in context of the surrounding days than found later in a separate record.

### Keeping the food notes simple

A brief note, "lighter appetite, skipped lunch" or "normal appetite, three meals," is more sustainable to keep up than a detailed calorie count for every entry. The goal is spotting patterns over weeks, not producing a precise food diary, and an overly demanding format is the most common reason this kind of log gets abandoned.

## Buying versus printing one

A bought diary suits someone who wants a durable, pre-bound format that won't fall apart over a multi-month course. A free printable template, adjusted to include exactly the columns wanted, suits anyone happy to print at home and file the pages in a folder. Neither is better in principle; the right choice depends on which format is more likely to actually get used every week.

### Digital combined logs

A shared spreadsheet or a tracking app with custom fields does the same job digitally, and makes it easier to see food notes and weight side by side over months rather than flipping pages. This suits anyone who already tracks other things digitally and doesn't want a second physical book to carry around.

## Where to buy one in the UK

General stationery chains, health and wellness sections of larger bookshops, and online retailers all stock relevant diaries, though few are branded specifically for GLP-1 use. A generic wellness journal with food and habit tracking columns works just as well, often at a lower price than one marketed narrowly for peptide logging.

## An example of a filled-in week

A typical combined entry might read: "Monday, 4mg dose, left thigh. Appetite noticeably lower Tuesday and Wednesday, back to normal by Friday. Weight 78.2kg, down 0.3kg from last week." This single entry captures the dose, a food-related observation, and the weight trend together, giving a fuller picture than any one of those three recorded alone.

### Reviewing food notes over a month

Scanning back through a month of food notes alongside the weight trend, rather than the dose entries alone, often reveals a clearer relationship between appetite changes and the weekly number than either record shows in isolation. This is one of the more useful reasons to keep both in the same place rather than split across separate books or apps.

### Adjusting the format as needs change

A combined diary bought or printed at the start of a course doesn't need to stay exactly the same for its full length. Adding a column partway through, for injection site rotation or a body measurement, is easy with a printed or digital format and doesn't require starting over, unlike a bound bought journal with a fixed layout.

### Keeping food notes free of judgement

Writing food notes in neutral, factual language, what was eaten and roughly when, rather than labelling meals as good or bad, keeps the diary a useful record rather than something that starts to feel like a source of guilt over time.

## A short buying checklist

Date, dose, site, weight and a brief food or appetite note as the core fields, a format (bought, printed, or digital) chosen based on what's likely to actually be kept up, and food notes kept brief enough to sustain every week rather than detailed enough to become a chore.
