## Working out how much to order

A 90-day course logged weekly covers roughly 12 to 13 weekly doses. Before ordering, working out the total mg needed across that period, based on a rough titration schedule if one's been sketched out, avoids running short partway through or over-ordering more than needed for three months.

### Ordering in stages versus all at once

Some buyers order a full 90-day supply upfront to lock in a price and avoid reordering. Others order in smaller batches, four to six weeks at a time, which costs slightly more per order but means less is tied up if the plan changes partway through. Either approach works; the choice comes down to budget and how confident the plan feels at the outset.

## What the listing should confirm before ordering

A COA-backed mg content figure for the specific batch, clear reconstitution guidance if the product needs it, and realistic UK dispatch and delivery timing are the three things worth confirming before committing to a 90-day order. A listing that's vague on any of these is harder to plan a full quarter's log around confidently.

### Storage over 90 days

Reconstituted retatrutide generally needs refrigeration and has a limited shelf life once mixed, so confirming the stated usable window against the 90-day plan matters, since buying more than can be used within that window risks waste.

## Structuring the log before day one

A 90-day log benefits from being set up in advance with a rough week-by-week structure: the expected dose for each stage, space for the actual dose given, injection site, weight, and notes. Building this template before the first dose arrives, even if it changes as the course unfolds, gives every entry after day one a reference point to sit alongside.

### Breaking 90 days into checkpoints

Rather than treating 90 days as one long stretch, breaking the log into three roughly monthly checkpoints, reviewing the trend at day 30, day 60, and day 90, makes a long log easier to actually look back on and less likely to become just a pile of unreviewed weekly numbers.

## A worked example for 90 days

The commonly used slow titration starts at 1mg, rises by 1mg each week, and settles into a 4mg maintenance dose from week four onward. On that basis, a 90-day, roughly 13-week course needs enough total mg to cover one week each at 1mg, 2mg, and 3mg, plus around ten weeks at the 4mg maintenance dose. Adding this up before ordering, rather than estimating loosely, avoids a mid-course shortfall or a large amount of unused product left over at the end. This is a general planning figure rather than a personal prescription; Enzo Meds' customer service team can help work out a schedule that fits your own course.

### Building in a small buffer

Ordering slightly more than the exact calculated total, enough to cover an extra week or two, is a reasonable buffer against a delayed delivery or a week where a dose needs repeating. This buffer is worth the small extra cost for the peace of mind of not running short partway through a 90-day plan.

## Reviewing the plan partway through

Setting a checkpoint around day 45, roughly the halfway point, to review whether the original 90-day supply order still matches how the course is actually unfolding is a sensible habit. If a dose ended up different from what was originally planned, this is the point to adjust a second order before running short, rather than discovering a shortfall in the final few weeks.

### What to do with a leftover supply at day 90

If the course ends with product still remaining, checking the stated storage life against how much time is left before deciding whether to continue, pause, or discard the remainder is worth doing rather than assuming leftover stock is automatically fine to use indefinitely.

### Keeping the maths simple

Writing the total weeks, total mg needed, and order quantity together on a single line before checkout, rather than trusting mental maths at the point of ordering, is a small habit that avoids a costly ordering mistake for a purchase this size.

## A short buying checklist

Total mg needed calculated against a rough 90-day schedule, order timing decided (upfront or staged), COA and reconstitution guidance confirmed on the listing, storage window checked against the full 90 days, and a log template with monthly checkpoints set up before the first dose arrives.
