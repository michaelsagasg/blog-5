## Consistency matters more than features

The single most important thing a scale needs for weekly weigh-ins is a consistent reading, not a long list of extra functions. A basic digital scale that gives the same number twice in a row, weighed a few seconds apart, is more useful for a log than an expensive scale with unreliable calibration. UK high street retailers and supermarkets sell basic digital bathroom scales from around £15 to £25, which is enough for most weekly tracking.

### What "consistent" actually means

Step on, note the number, step off, step back on within a minute. If the reading matches within 0.1kg to 0.2kg both times, the scale is reliable enough for weekly use. A scale that swings by half a kilo or more between back-to-back weighings isn't worth keeping for a log, however cheap it was.

## Where to buy one

Argos, Boots, and most large supermarkets in the UK stock a range of digital bathroom scales in store, useful for anyone who wants to check the display and build quality before buying. Online retailers offer a wider range, including scales with body composition readings, at prices from around £15 up to £60 or more for models with app syncing.

### Features worth paying extra for

A memory function that stores the last reading, a large display that's easy to read first thing in the morning, and a flat, stable base on a hard floor are worth the small price jump over the very cheapest models. Body fat or muscle mass readings are a bonus but shouldn't be the deciding factor, since these estimates vary more between devices than weight alone.

## Setting it up for reliable weekly readings

A scale placed on a hard, level surface gives more consistent readings than one on carpet, where the base can sink slightly and throw off the number. Weighing at a similar time of day each week, most commonly first thing in the morning before eating or drinking, removes a lot of the noise that comes from normal daily fluctuation rather than an actual change.

### Where this fits in the log

The weight entry is only one line in a weekly log, but it's the line most people check first. Recording the exact time of day and any relevant context, a late night, a heavier meal the evening before, next to the number itself makes the trend easier to read honestly a few weeks later, rather than reacting to a single number in isolation.

## Smart scales, if the budget allows

Scales that sync to a phone app automatically log the weekly number without needing to write it down by hand, which some people find keeps the habit going longer. These typically cost £30 to £50 in the UK and connect over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi. They're a convenience, not a requirement, since a basic scale and a notebook do exactly the same job for anyone who prefers writing it down.

## Common scale-buying mistakes

Buying based on the lowest price alone, without checking a return policy, is a common mistake, since even a reputable brand occasionally sells a faulty unit. UK retailers like Argos and Boots generally offer straightforward returns within 30 days, which is worth confirming before buying online from a less familiar seller. Another common mistake is assuming a more expensive scale is automatically more accurate; price in this category mostly buys extra features, not core weight accuracy.

### Replacing an old scale

If replacing an existing scale partway through a log, running both scales side by side for a week or two before switching over fully helps establish whether the new scale reads meaningfully differently from the old one. Noting this transition clearly in the log avoids an unexplained jump in the trend that's actually just a scale change.

### A final note on longevity

A well-made basic scale, kept on a stable surface and given fresh batteries when needed, typically lasts several years of weekly use, making the upfront cost of £15 to £25 a reasonable one-off investment rather than a recurring expense.

## A short buying checklist

A scale that reads consistently within 0.1 to 0.2kg on a repeat weighing, placed on a hard level surface, used at a similar time each week, with a memory function or app sync as a nice extra rather than a necessity. Spending more only makes sense once the basics, consistency and a stable surface, are already covered.
