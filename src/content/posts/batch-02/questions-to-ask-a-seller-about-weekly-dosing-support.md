## Does the listing show a general dosing schedule

The first question worth asking, or checking before needing to ask, is whether the product listing includes a general weekly dosing schedule showing a typical starting dose and how it might step up over a course. Some UK sellers, Enzo Meds among them, include this directly on the product page, which saves the question entirely.

### What to do if it's not shown

If a listing doesn't show a schedule, messaging the seller to ask for a general starting dose and typical step interval is a reasonable question to put directly. A seller who answers plainly and specifically, rather than vaguely, is a good sign for the rest of the buying relationship.

## Is the mg content backed by a certificate of analysis

Asking whether a current, batch-specific certificate of analysis is available for the exact vial or pen being purchased, not just a generic COA for the product line, confirms the mg figure a whole log will be built around is actually verified.

### Follow-up if a COA isn't offered upfront

If a COA isn't shown on the listing itself, asking for it before ordering, rather than after, gives a chance to decide whether to proceed with more confidence in the numbers.

## What supplies are included with the order

Asking specifically whether syringes, alcohol swabs, or bacteriostatic water ship with the order avoids assuming something is included when it isn't, which is one of the most common gaps between what a buyer expects and what actually arrives.

### Reconstitution guidance, if relevant

For a product needing reconstitution, asking directly what water volume is suggested and what concentration results removes a calculation a first-time buyer would otherwise have to work out alone.

## Dispatch and delivery timing

Asking for a realistic dispatch cutoff and expected UK delivery window lets a buyer plan exactly when the first dose, and the first log entry, will happen, rather than guessing at a start date.

### What a vague answer suggests

A seller unwilling or unable to give a rough delivery estimate is worth treating with some caution, since even variable delivery times can usually be described with a reasonable range.

## Putting the answers to use

Every answer gathered here, the schedule, the COA, the included supplies, the reconstitution guidance, the delivery timing, feeds directly into setting up a log's first few entries accurately. Asking these questions before ordering, rather than working it out after the first vial arrives, means the log starts on solid ground.

## How to phrase these questions clearly

Asking specific, numbered questions in a single message, rather than one vague general question, tends to get a more useful response. A message listing "1. Is a COA available for this batch? 2. What supplies are included? 3. What's the expected UK delivery time?" is easier for a seller to answer completely than an open-ended "can you tell me more about this product?"

### What a good response looks like

A strong response answers each question directly and specifically, with numbers and dates rather than general reassurance. A response like "COA attached, delivery typically 2 to 3 working days, syringes and swabs included" gives far more to work with than a vague "yes, everything's covered."

## Saving the answers for later reference

Keeping a copy of the seller's responses, screenshotted or saved as a note, alongside the rest of the order details, means the information is still available months later if a question comes up again, rather than needing to message the seller a second time for something already answered once.

### A final practical note

Most of this takes only a few minutes to ask and answer before an order is placed, a small time cost that's easily worth it against the confusion an unclear first order can cause later.

### One last thing to remember

None of these questions are unreasonable to ask. A legitimate UK seller expects and welcomes them from anyone placing a first order.

## A short checklist of questions

Is a general dosing schedule shown or available. Is the COA current and batch-specific. What supplies are included. What reconstitution guidance is given. What's the realistic UK delivery window. A seller who answers all five clearly is generally a safer first order than one who leaves several vague.
