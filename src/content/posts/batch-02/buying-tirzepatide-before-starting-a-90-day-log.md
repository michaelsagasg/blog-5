## Working out how much to order

A 90-day course logged weekly covers roughly 12 to 13 weekly doses. Before ordering, calculating the total mg needed across that period, based on a rough titration schedule if one has been sketched out, avoids running short partway through or ordering more than a full quarter actually needs.

### Ordering upfront versus in stages

Some buyers order the full 90-day supply in one go to lock in pricing and avoid a second order. Others prefer ordering in smaller batches, four to six weeks at a time, which costs a little more overall but leaves less committed if a plan changes along the way. Both are reasonable; the right choice depends on budget and confidence in the plan at the outset.

## What the listing should confirm first

A COA-backed mg content figure for the specific batch, clear reconstitution guidance where the product needs it, and honest UK dispatch and delivery timing are worth confirming before committing to a 90-day order. A listing vague on any of these makes planning a full quarter's log harder than it needs to be.

### Storage across 90 days

Reconstituted tirzepatide generally needs refrigeration and has a limited usable window once mixed, so checking that window against the 90-day plan matters, since buying more than can realistically be used within it risks waste.

## Structuring the log before day one

A 90-day log is easier to keep up if it's set up in advance with a rough week-by-week structure: the expected dose at each stage, space for the actual dose given, injection site, weight, and a notes column. Sketching this out before the first dose arrives, even loosely, gives every entry from day one a reference point rather than a blank page each week.

### Breaking 90 days into checkpoints

Treating 90 days as three rough monthly checkpoints, reviewing the trend at day 30, 60, and 90, makes a long log easier to actually review, rather than letting weekly entries pile up unreviewed until the very end.

## A worked example for 90 days

If a rough schedule suggests starting at 2.5mg and stepping up every four weeks toward 5mg then 7.5mg, a 90-day, roughly 13-week course needs enough total mg to cover four weeks at 2.5mg, four weeks at 5mg, and around five weeks at 7.5mg. Working this out before ordering, rather than estimating loosely, avoids either a shortfall partway through or a large surplus left over at the end of the course.

### Building in a small buffer

Ordering a little more than the exact calculated total, enough to cover an extra week or two, is a sensible buffer against a delayed delivery or a week needing a repeat dose. This small extra cost is generally worth it for not running short midway through a 90-day plan.

## Reviewing the plan partway through

Setting a checkpoint around day 45, roughly halfway, to compare the original 90-day supply order against how the actual course has unfolded is a sensible habit. If the real dose ended up different from the initial plan, this is the point to adjust a follow-up order before running short in the final weeks rather than after.

### Handling leftover supply at the end

If the course finishes with product still remaining, checking the stated storage life against the remaining time before deciding whether to continue, pause, or safely discard the surplus is worth doing rather than assuming leftover stock stays usable indefinitely.

### Keeping the maths visible

Writing the total weeks, total mg needed, and order quantity together on a single line at checkout, rather than relying on mental maths, is a small habit that avoids a costly mistake on an order of this size.

### A final word on patience

A 90-day plan is a marathon, not a sprint. Getting the supply order right at the outset removes one variable from a course that will already have enough natural variation to track and interpret along the way.

## A short buying checklist

Total mg needed worked out against a rough 90-day schedule, order timing decided (upfront or staged), COA and reconstitution guidance confirmed on the listing, the storage window checked against the full 90 days, and a log template with monthly checkpoints ready before the first dose is due.
